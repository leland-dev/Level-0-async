Lesson 2 · Create Images and Video with AI

One idea, two new kinds of output: pictures and video.

What you'll learn

Concept

Images and video, same idea

Images: it predicts pixels the same way it predicts words.

An image model has looked at a huge number of images and learned the patterns in what things tend to look like. When you describe a subject and a style, it generates a brand new grid of pixels that fits your description, guided by that learned pattern. Nothing gets pulled up from a library of real photos. The whole image is predicted, the same idea as text, just applied to pixels instead of words.

Because it's generating an image rather than pulling up a real one, be thoughtful about what you create and share, especially anything that could be mistaken for a real photo or person. More on using this responsibly in Lesson 6.

Video: turning a description into a short clip

The same prediction idea extends to motion. Give a video model a text description, or a short storyboard of what should happen, and it generates a short clip frame by frame to match. Today that means a few seconds of footage, not a full scene or a movie. Motion can still look imperfect and details can drift between frames, so treat it as a fast way to sketch an idea in motion, not a finished production tool yet.

  • Image generation works from a text description, not a real photo you upload
  • Specific style and format words in your prompt change the pixels it predicts
  • Video generation today means short clips, and it's still improving fast

Build 1 · Setup

Get set up

Open ChatGPT (chatgpt.com). It generates images directly in the chat, no separate tool needed. Pick a subject you actually want to see rendered, a pet, a place, an object, anything.

Copy the madlibs prompt below into ChatGPT, filling in your own subject and style.

[subject] in the style of [style], [mood/lighting], [format]

Worked example: "A treehouse in a redwood forest, in the style of a Studio Ghibli film, golden hour lighting, wide shot."

Why this prompt works

Each bracket does a different job. The subject tells it what to draw. The style name anchors it to a specific learned look instead of a generic default. Mood and lighting shape the atmosphere. Format (wide shot, close-up, portrait) controls the framing, the same way it would for a photographer.

Look at what comes back. Check that the subject, style, and framing all match what you asked for.

🔧 Troubleshooting: the result doesn't match what you pictured

Add more specific style and format language. "Illustration" is vague, "flat vector illustration with thick outlines" gives it something concrete to aim for.

Build 2 · Stretch

Go further: generate a video (optional)

Video generation is newer and less reliable than images, so treat this as exploratory. Try it in whichever video tool you have access to.

⚡ Try a video generation prompt 
A [subject] doing [motion], [duration] seconds, [style]. Example: "A paper airplane gliding through a sunlit office, 4 seconds, stop-motion animation style."

Expect a short, rough clip rather than something polished. Motion can wobble and small details can shift between frames. That's the current state of the tech, not a sign you did anything wrong.

⚡ Try AI music generation

Describe a genre and a mood to a music generator like Suno, and see what a 30-second track sounds like from just a text prompt.

⚡ Try an AI-assisted game

Some browser games now generate levels, dialogue, or art on the fly instead of shipping it all pre-made. Worth poking at one just to see where the generation shows up.
