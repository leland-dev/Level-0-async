Lesson 3 · Put AI to Work on Your Actual Life

Concept

Same AI, different job

The idea, one word at a time

|

Modes

AI can take on a few different jobs depending on what you ask for. Naming the job gets you a better answer than just typing your task and hoping.

  • Reviewer or editor. Checks or improves something you already made.
  • Thought partner. Talks through a decision with you, out loud.
  • Creator. Makes something from scratch when you're starting with nothing.
  • Researcher. Gathers and synthesizes information you don't already have.

Mapping your work

Not every task is worth handing to AI. The ones that usually are share a few traits.

  • It's repetitive: you've done a version of this task many times before
  • It's time-consuming but low-judgment: it takes a while, but doesn't need much of your specific expertise
  • It's something you'd delegate to a capable assistant, if you had one sitting next to you

Build 1 · Setup

Pick a real task

Think of one real task from your actual life this week: something you need to write, decide, make, or find out. Not a hypothetical, an actual thing on your plate.

Now decide which mode fits: reviewer/editor, thought partner, creator, or researcher.

Copy the prompt below into your AI tool, swapping in your own mode and task from the last page.

Act as a [reviewer/editor, thought partner, creator, or researcher] for this: [describe your actual task in 1-2 sentences]. If you already have something written, I'll paste it below. If not, ask me what I'm trying to produce or decide before you start.
Why this prompt works

Naming the mode explicitly changes the kind of reply you get. Without it, AI defaults to just answering. A thought partner should ask you questions back before it weighs in. A creator should just produce a draft. Say which one you want.

Read what comes back. If it jumped straight to an answer but you wanted thought-partner mode, that's a sign to nudge it.

🔧 Troubleshooting: it just answered instead of thinking with you

Add a line: "Ask me clarifying questions before you respond." That one sentence is usually enough to shift it out of answer-mode.

Same four modes from the concept page. Naming one turns a generic reply into the kind of help you actually needed.

Build 2 · Map your work

Map your own recurring work

List a handful of tasks you do regularly: weekly reports, scheduling, note-taking, first drafts, whatever comes up again and again. For each one, tag it AI-suited or not, using the traits from the concept page. Then note which mode it calls for.

If your list feels thin, have AI interview you. It's often better at spotting patterns in your own week than you are, since you're too close to it.

Interview me about my typical week and help me identify which of my tasks could be handed to an AI assistant. Ask me questions one at a time about what I do regularly, then summarize which tasks look AI-suited and which mode (reviewer/editor, thought partner, creator, or researcher) fits each one.
Why this prompt works

Being interviewed surfaces tasks you wouldn't think to list on your own. It's easy to forget the small recurring things once you're not staring at your calendar, and a question-by-question format catches more of them than a blank list ever does.

Quick self-check: which best describes most of your recurring tasks?
