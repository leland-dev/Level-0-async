Lesson 6 · Classify and Sanitize Your Data

Not everything belongs in an AI chat. Knowing the difference is a skill, not a guess.

What you'll learn

  • A simple three-tier way to classify anything before you put it into AI: public, internal, or sensitive
  • How to strip sensitive details out of something before you share it
  • How to sanitize a real document and check what you'd have lost by doing it

Concept

Classify it, then sanitize what's left

1. Classification: sort what you're about to share into public, internal, or sensitive.

Public is fine anywhere: a blog draft, a public job post, a general question. Internal is fine for an approved work tool but shouldn't leave the company: a roadmap, an internal process doc, numbers that aren't announced yet. Sensitive is personal data, credentials, or anything that shouldn't leave a controlled system: someone's SSN, a password, medical or financial detail.

What happens to what you type varies by tool and by your account settings. Some tools train on your conversations by default, some don't, and enterprise or workspace settings can turn that off. Check your settings instead of assuming.

The whole check, in one line

|

2. Sanitizing: strip the sensitive parts before you share the rest.

Sanitizing means removing personally identifiable information, PII, before you paste something in: names, emails, phone numbers, account or ID numbers. A regular chat and a "ring-fenced" project or workspace with tighter data handling aren't the same thing. A ring-fenced setup can keep what you share out of training and away from other users, but it's a setting someone configured, not a guarantee. Sanitize anyway.

  • A document can look internal on the surface and still have something sensitive buried inside it, like an SSN in a roadmap appendix
  • "My company's plan already covers this" is worth checking, not assuming
  • When you're not sure which tier something is, treat it as the more sensitive one

Build 1 · Classify

Sort five real things you've put into AI this week

Think back through the last few days. Pick five things you actually typed or pasted into an AI tool, from a quick question to a full document. Sort each one into public, internal, or sensitive.

  • A question with no company or personal detail in it
  • Something work-related you'd only paste into an approved work tool
  • Anything that touched a real person's private information

If anything lands in sensitive and you're not sure it should have gone in, open your AI tool's settings and check what it actually does with your data. Adjust the setting if it needs adjusting.

Pick a real spreadsheet or document you have handy, something with a few personal details in it: a customer list, a contact sheet, an expense report. Make a sanitized copy first: swap names, emails, and any account or ID numbers for placeholders like [NAME] and [EMAIL]. Keep the structure and the numbers that actually matter.

Here's a sanitized version of [describe your document, e.g. "a customer list" or "an expense report"], with names and emails replaced by placeholders. Summarize the main patterns you see and flag anything that looks unusual: [paste your sanitized data]
Why this works

Sanitizing first means you still get the value, the summary, the pattern-spotting, the analysis, without ever putting a real person's information into a chat you don't fully control.

Run it, then look at the raw version yourself and ask whether sanitizing cost the AI anything it needed. Most of the time it doesn't. The totals, patterns, and outliers are all still there. Only the names are gone.

🔧 Troubleshooting: it keeps asking for names to make sense of the data

It doesn't need them. Tell it to refer to entries by row number or a placeholder ID instead.
