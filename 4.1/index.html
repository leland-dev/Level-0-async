Lesson 4 · Give AI Context: Upload Files

AI is a lot more useful once it can see the real thing, not just your description of it.

What you'll learn

  • How AI actually reads a file you upload, and what it does with it
  • What's safe to upload and what you should keep off of it
  • How to get real value out of a document, a photo, and a spreadsheet

Concept

Show it the file instead of describing it

Upload a file and AI reads all of it, instead of guessing from what you typed.

Once it has the actual content in front of it, it can work with what's really there, not with your summary of it.

The one-line version of this whole lesson

|

Documents

Upload a resume, a draft, a contract, whatever, and AI reads the full text into what it's working with. It can quote a specific line back to you, summarize the whole thing, or tell you what's weak in it. It's reacting to your actual words, not to a paraphrase you typed in the chat box.

Images and spreadsheets

Same idea, two more file types. Show it a photo and it can describe what's in the frame or pull out a specific detail, like the total on a receipt or the text on a whiteboard. Give it a spreadsheet and it can summarize what's in the rows or point out a pattern, without you retyping any of the data yourself.

  • Very long files sometimes get summarized rather than read in full, so it's worth checking what it actually picked up
  • A scanned image saved as a PDF can trip it up. Plain text or a real, selectable PDF works better
  • Say what you want done with the file. Attaching it and asking "thoughts?" gets a weaker answer than a specific ask

Build 1 · Setup

Get a document ready

Open your AI tool of choice: claude.ai, chatgpt.com, or gemini.google.com. Then find a real document you want a second opinion on. A resume, a draft you're revising, a plan you're not sure holds up. Something with actual flaws beats something already polished, since that's where AI's feedback is worth the most.

Not ready to upload something of your own? That's fine. Download the practice file below instead.

Practice file: a deliberately messy resume

Not a real person. Built to have real problems: inconsistent formatting, vague bullet points that don't show any real impact, an unexplained gap, and a couple of typos. Good material for practicing a critique, since there's plenty to actually find.

Upload your document (or the sample), then paste the prompt below, swapping in what it actually is.

Here's my [resume / draft / plan]. Review it like a critical, experienced [hiring manager / editor / advisor] would. Tell me: the 3 biggest weaknesses, one sentence each. What's genuinely working, if anything. One concrete rewrite of the single weakest section. Be specific and blunt. Don't just tell me it looks fine.
Why this prompt works

Naming a specific critical role (hiring manager, editor) pushes the response past generic praise. Asking for the weaknesses before anything else is what actually gets you feedback worth acting on.

Read the feedback against the actual document. It has your real words in front of it now, not a summary of them, so a specific critique is a good sign it actually read the thing.

🔧 Troubleshooting: the feedback is all compliments

Push back directly: "Stop being nice. What would make a reviewer reject this?" Most tools get noticeably sharper on the second ask.

Build 2 · Images and data

Same idea, two more file types. These are quick, since you already did the real work above.

Upload a photo

Find a photo with real information sitting in it: a receipt, a photo of a whiteboard from a meeting, a screenshot of a text thread. Upload it and ask AI to read it for you.

This is a photo of a receipt (or whiteboard, or screenshot). List everything on it as plain text: items, amounts, dates, names, whatever's there. Flag anything you genuinely can't read clearly instead of guessing at it.

Upload a spreadsheet

Upload a spreadsheet you already have. A budget, an expense log, a tracker, anything with real rows in it.

Summarize this spreadsheet in plain language. What are the 3 biggest patterns or outliers in it? Point to specific rows or columns for each one, don't just describe the sheet in general terms.
